## Problem

Small business owners who want to sell online usually face two bad options: build a custom store they can't afford to maintain, or list on a marketplace where they don't own their storefront, their customers or their brand. They need their own store, with its own link and its own dashboard, without needing a developer.

## Solution

Dokany is a multi-tenant SaaS e-commerce platform. Every seller gets an independent storefront on its own subdomain and a dedicated control panel, while all stores run on one shared, scalable backend.

- **Store creation in minutes** — a React Native mobile app lets a seller create a store in under 5 minutes and manage it from their phone.
- **Per-seller storefronts** — a dynamic storefront system serves separate themes and data for each seller.
- **Payments** — Stripe integration for secure online checkout.
- **Orders and commissions** — order management plus commission tracking between sellers and the platform.
- **Platform admin** — a dashboard to manage sellers, admins, themes, withdrawals and email campaigns.
- **Email** — transactional emails (order confirmations, shipping updates, customer notifications), targeted marketing campaigns by theme, city or country, and automated engagement sequences.

## Architecture

- **Backend** — Node.js and Express on PostgreSQL, with authentication and authorization shared by every tenant.
- **Multi-tenancy** — one shared backend serving many stores, each with its own subdomain, storefront theme and data.
- **Clients** — React web apps for the storefronts and dashboards (styled with Tailwind CSS and Bootstrap) and a React Native app for sellers.
- **Email infrastructure** — a custom mail server set up through Namecheap with dedicated addresses per purpose: `support@`, `seller-support@`, `admin@` and `marketing@`.

## Challenges

- **One backend, many stores.** Every query, theme and email has to be scoped to the right seller, while new stores keep appearing without any deployment.
- **Money between three parties.** Customers pay through Stripe, the platform takes a commission and sellers request withdrawals, so orders, commissions and payouts have to stay consistent.
- **Email that actually arrives.** Running a custom mail server with separate addresses for support, sellers, admins and marketing, across both transactional and bulk campaign email.
